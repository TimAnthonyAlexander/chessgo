## Why `build_half` jumped <0.8% → 6.24% (the important correction)

The 20Jul profile ran a middlegame-heavy batch where LAZYACC deferred nearly all king-cross
refreshes. This batch has **2 endgames at depth 22** — kings move constantly → many king-bucket
crossings **that reach an eval** → `build_half` fires for real. **Lesson: `build_half`'s share is
strongly workload-dependent (king-activity-dependent); the "<0.8%, ~dead" reading was a
middlegame artifact, not a property of the engine.** Real-game share sits between the two,
weighted by endgame frequency. → The refresh-breakdown measurement (below) sizes the *cacheable*
part on a realistic mix.

## Lever map (retrain-free, current)

1. **`apply_diff` 19.3%** — threat-column bandwidth. Retrain-free levers (LAZYACC/LAZYACC2)
   shipped. The remaining lever is **int8 threat FT via QAT — Post-Train (August)**, ~2× the
   dominant streamed bandwidth.
2. **`build_half` 6.24%** — the king-cross refresh. **Two retrain-free levers, both around this:**
   - **P1 / Finny** caches the **base (PSQ) half** of the refresh (pre-check #2: SF does NOT cache
     threats). Ceiling = base-feature share of `build_half` — being measured.
   - **THREATGATE (already implemented, default-OFF)** cuts **threat** refreshes to mirror-flips
     only (same-mirror bucket cross keeps threats on the cheap delta path). Potentially the bigger
     of the two, since it attacks the threat portion of the refresh. **Quick to evaluate — bench
     NPS + golden-check for byte-identity.**
3. **`eval_from_halves` 11.6%** — L1 dot is a fraction of this (rest = pairwise activation + L2/L3
   + round). **P2 / sparse-affine** skips ~50% of L1 blocks as-is (pre-check #1), ~96% if
   permuted. Modest ceiling; permute-then-sparse is the real form.
4. **`roundf32` 2.5%** — eval-tail round (ROUNDFAST washed before; low priority).
5. Search overhead (negamax/score_moves/movegen/TT ≈ 15%) — the search campaign's turf (P4–P8).
