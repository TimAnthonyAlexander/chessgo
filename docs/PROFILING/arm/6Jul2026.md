Eval is ~80% of all engine CPU. It splits into accumulator maintenance (the FT
column add/sub kernels + the king-bucket rebuild) and the forward pass
(`evalFromHalvesLean`, ~9.5%). The int8 FT column add/sub kernels plus their
inlined Load/Store are ~half the entire process. HCE is completely absent — NNUE
is the sole eval. Biggest lever: cut columns-touched-per-node (better deltas,
avoid the 25%-cum king-refresh `buildAccHalf`) or widen those kernels.

Pure-search machinery is ~5% flat. ~91% is delegated NNUE (scalar-inflated —
that's the split: accumulator update on make-move (76.9% cum) dwarfs leaf
Evaluate (14.5%) ~5:1 — the move-aware net patches FT halves on every push.
Within actual search logic, move ordering/selection (`selectMoveLegacy`) is the
only piece with meaningful self-time; SEE, movegen, TT, and un-make are all
near-free.

## Caveats on both

- Eval profile is arm64/NEON — on prod amd64/AVX-512 the FT kernels are
  wider/cheaper, so the forward pass and the edge-diff bookkeeping take a
  relatively larger share.
- Search profile is scalar-arm64 — search-function attribution is valid, but the
  eval/search ratio is not representative of prod.
- Both point to the same conclusion: the accumulator/FT-column path is the
  dominant cost, and the make-move incremental update matters ~5× more than the
  leaf forward pass. Confirm the exact amd64 split with a `bench nps -cpuprofile`
  run or prod.
